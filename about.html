The name is not decoration. It is the whole argument.

There are not two features here. There is one month of your spending, read twice. The reading that unsettles people and the reading that pleases them would be built from the same purchases, in the same proportions, on the same day. The first reading is what the spending half does today. The second is a preview: the investing half is not built yet. It would be worthless if you did not trust the first.

THE LOGO ALREADY SAYS IT

Cards and charts on one side. Company stock on the other (the investing half, not built yet). The ghost in the middle, holding a bank in its bag and a phone in its hand, with a line rising from one side to the other. There is no second explanation to invent: the picture and the product are the same idea, and the two halves were never meant to be separate things.

WHY THE ORDER MATTERS

The spending half comes first and stands alone. Not as a funnel — as the evidence. Nobody should let software pick what they own on the strength of a categorisation they have not checked. That is why the spending half comes first, and why the investing half is not built until the first reading can be trusted.

A LINE WE WILL NOT CROSS

The number startles. We don't.

An app that sets out to unsettle you is one bad decision away from lecturing you, and we would rather lose the joke than become that. So the first reading is arithmetic and nothing else. There is no score, no traffic light, no comparison with “people like you”, no red on a total, and no copy anywhere suggesting what you should have done with your own money.

And the second reading, once it exists, is not a prize for feeling bad. It would be offered the same way on a month you are pleased with as on one you are not — because it is the same calculation either way, and pretending otherwise would be a trick of a different kind.

WHAT YOUR BANK SENDS US
  • The date, amount and merchant of each purchasePlus the category your bank assigned it, which is how a transfer gets told apart from a coffee. Connecting a bank shares transactions with Trick or Treat through Plaid.
  • Which account each purchase came fromName, last four digits, and whether it is a card, a current account or savings.
  • Corrections, when a pending charge settlesA $4 hold that posts at $4.60 usually arrives as an amendment, not a second purchase.
WHAT IT DOES NOT ASK FOR
  • Any way to move moneyTrick or Treat asks Plaid for transactions only, and has no code that moves money.
  • Your banking passwordYou type it at your bank or at Plaid, never here. We receive a token instead, and it is kept in its own encrypted table, apart from your transactions.
  • To keep reading after you disconnectDisconnecting stops syncing as soon as you disconnect. Your data for that bank is deleted once Plaid confirms the disconnect.

The three hard parts of the first reading

Drawing a chart of your categories is easy. These are the reasons most spending apps show a number that is quietly wrong — and a wrong first reading makes the second one worse than useless.

ONE

Your own money, counted as spending

Paying a card from your current account is one movement on two statements. We match the pair — same amount, within four days, out of a current account and into a card — and remove both sides. Every removal is listed with its reason. The pairing is our inference and can be wrong.

TWO

Numbers that change after you read them

A pending charge can settle at a different amount days later, so last week's total is not final. We show pending charges and mark them, so a total that can still move is not presented as final.

THREE

A picture missing one of its sources

If a bank stops syncing, the charts still look perfectly normal — they are just wrong, and nothing would tell you. Each bank connection carries a status, so a stale one can be spotted and named. Showing the total as a floor when a bank is stale is planned, not built yet.

And what the second reading would honestly be worth

Preview only. None of this is built yet, and nothing here can be bought.

Just over half of it maps to anything

Your landlord is not listed on an exchange, and neither is the restaurant on the corner. In the sample September above, $2,489 of $4,450 could be matched to a company you can buy. Matching real merchants to companies is not built yet. A basket built from that would be a slice of a life, not a portrait of one.

Nothing would be bought before you have seen it

The plan is that showing a basket is the default and buying it is a separate, deliberate act. None of that is built yet, so nothing can be bought here. Spending where you already spend is also not a strategy anyone has shown to work; it is a way of owning what you use.

The money would sit behind its own wall

The plan is a separate account you fund and cap yourself. It does not exist yet, and today nothing in Trick or Treat moves money.
