The name is not decoration. It is the whole argument.

There are not two features here. There is one month of your spending, read twice. The reading that unsettles people and the reading that pleases them are built from the same purchases, in the same proportions, on the same day. If you understand that, you understand the product — and you can also see why the second reading would be worthless if you did not trust the first.

THE LOGO ALREADY SAYS IT

Cards and charts on one side. Company stock on the other. The ghost in the middle, holding a bank in its bag and a phone in its hand, with a line rising from one side to the other. There is no second explanation to invent: the picture and the product are the same idea, and the two halves were never meant to be separate things.

WHY THE ORDER MATTERS

The spending half comes first and stands alone, free and complete. Not as a funnel — as the evidence. Nobody should let software pick what they own on the strength of a categorisation they have not checked. So you get to disagree with the first reading, correct it, and watch it change, before the second one is ever offered to you.

A LINE WE WILL NOT CROSS

The number startles. We don't.

An app that sets out to unsettle you is one bad decision away from lecturing you, and we would rather lose the joke than become that. So the first reading is arithmetic and nothing else. There is no score, no traffic light, no comparison with “people like you”, no red on a total, and no copy anywhere suggesting what you should have done with your own money.

And the second reading is not a prize for feeling bad. It is offered the same way on a month you are pleased with as on one you are not — because it is the same calculation either way, and pretending otherwise would be a trick of a different kind.

WHAT YOUR BANK SENDS US
  • The date, amount and merchant of each purchasePlus the category your bank assigned it, which is how a transfer gets told apart from a coffee.
  • Which account each purchase came fromName, last four digits, and whether it is a card, a current account or savings.
  • Corrections, when a pending charge settlesA $4 hold that posts at $4.60 arrives as an amendment, not a second purchase.
WHAT IT CANNOT ASK FOR
  • The ability to move a centThe bank connection requests transaction history and only that — in both halves of the app, including the investing one.
  • Your banking passwordYou type it at your bank or at Plaid, never here. We receive a token instead, and it is kept apart from your transactions.
  • To keep reading after you disconnectDisconnecting stops the sync that day and deletes that bank's transactions.

The three hard parts of the first reading

Drawing a chart of your categories is easy. These are the reasons most spending apps show a number that is quietly wrong — and a wrong first reading makes the second one worse than useless.

ONE

Your own money, counted as spending

Paying a card from your current account is one movement on two statements. We match the pair — same amount, within four days, out of a current account and into a card — and remove both sides. Every removal is listed with its reason, and you can put one back.

TWO

Numbers that change after you read them

A pending charge can settle at a different amount days later, so last week's total is not final. We show pending charges, mark them, and tell you when a past month moves — instead of restating history quietly and hoping you don't notice.

THREE

A picture missing one of its sources

If a bank stops syncing, the charts still look perfectly normal — they are just wrong, and nothing would tell you. So a stale connection marks the total as a floor, withholds every comparison, and names the bank. The same gate refuses to build a basket at all.

And what the second reading is honestly worth

Under two fifths of it maps to anything

Your landlord is not listed on an exchange, and neither is the restaurant on the corner. In the September above, $1,835 of $4,813 could be matched to a company you can buy. A basket built from that is a narrow slice of a life, not a portrait of one, and it is shown as such.

Nothing is bought before you have seen it

Showing the basket is the default and buying it is a separate, deliberate act — that order exists in the code rather than in a promise. Spending where you already spend is also not a strategy anyone has shown to work; it is a way of owning what you use.

The money sits behind its own wall

It would use a separate account you fund and cap yourself, and nothing here can move money into or out of it. Switching the second half off leaves the first untouched, and never starting it costs you nothing at all.

Every limit on this page is repeated inside the account, on the screen where a bank is connected — with your own institution named, before anything has been read.
